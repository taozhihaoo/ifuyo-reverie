Why I Keep a Reading Archive

Jane Doe

Ten years ago I lost three hundred saved articles when a read-later service shut down. Since then I keep my own archive: plain files, on my own disk.

A folder is a promise. Databases come and go, services pivot and die, but a directory of Markdown files with a metadata JSON next to each one will still open in twenty years.

Your library is a folder, not an account.

The workflow is simple. Anything worth reading twice gets saved, annotated, and indexed. The index is disposable; the files are the truth.

This post was inspired by losing data exactly once.
